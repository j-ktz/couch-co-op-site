Why keep a record of your days?

Many states and countries use the number of days you spend there to decide whether you count as a resident for tax purposes. The rules differ from place to place, and you may be asked to show where you were on particular days. Memory and a rough spreadsheet are hard to rely on. A day-by-day record, with where each entry came from, is much easier to stand behind.

Statecount keeps that record for you on your phone, tells you how many days you have left before a limit you set, and never sends your location anywhere.

Statecount doesn't know which rules apply to you. Ask a tax professional about your situation.

Questions people ask

How does Statecount know which state I'm in?

Your iPhone's location is matched, on the phone, against map boundaries built into the app (US Census and Natural Earth data). The app makes no network calls of its own.

Does my location leave my phone?

No. There's no account, no server and no analytics, and the App Store privacy label is Data Not Collected. If you turn on iCloud sync, your days sync through your own private iCloud.

What counts as a day?

Statecount records every state or country you were in on each date, so a travel day shows both places. How a day counts depends on the rule. New York's tax department, for example, says any part of a day is a day. Your tax professional can tell you how a rule applies to you.

Can I fix or add days?

Yes. You can add or edit any day by hand. Each record shows whether it came from your phone's location or from you, and days with no record are flagged so you can fill them in.

What's in the report?

Every day of the year and where it was recorded, exported as a PDF and a CSV you can share with your tax professional.

Does it work outside the US?

Statecount records countries as well as US states, so a week in Canada shows as Canada.

Is it free?

Yes, to log by hand with one limit for the current year. Statecount Pro is $29.99 a year with a 7-day free trial, or $59.99 once, and adds automatic logging, as many limits as you need, every year of history, widgets and alerts.

Is Statecount tax advice?

No. Statecount keeps records. It is not tax or legal advice, and it doesn't know which rules apply to you.
